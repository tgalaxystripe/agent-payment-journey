Method, and what these numbers do and don’t show
  • What a run is. One agent, one real browser, one real merchant site, one real card, told to buy one specific item under a spending cap. A run counts as a success only on a merchant order confirmation — not on the agent’s own say-so.
  • What “advice” is. A short, merchant-specific playbook appended to the prompt: the checkout’s URL sequence, its known traps, how its confirmation page looks, and when to ask the human. It is assembled from what previous agents observed on that site. Nothing about the model, tools, or permissions changes between arms.
  • The paired numbers are the honest ones. The two arms aren’t perfectly balanced (46 unadvised vs 34 advised runs across several rounds), so the headline rates can drift on merchant mix. Restricting to the 28 cases where the same task ran both ways gives 20/28 → 24/28. Advice fixed 5 runs and broke 1.
  • Human approval time is excluded from working time. Every purchase pauses for a human to approve the wallet spend request. That wait ranged from 11 seconds to 10 minutes and is not the agent’s doing, so the time figures count only the agent’s own active work.
  • Caveats. Small n. Live commercial sites that change under you. Two model versions across rounds. Merchants were chosen because agents already struggled there, so the unadvised baseline is not a general “agents can’t check out” rate. All three journeys shown are cases where the unadvised run failed — the interesting case, not the typical one; unadvised runs completed 67% of the time overall.
