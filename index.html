Method, and what these numbers do and don’t show
  • What a run is. One agent with a real browser, a real merchant site and a real card, told to buy a specific item under a spending cap. It counts as a success only when the merchant shows an order confirmation, not when the agent says it succeeded.
  • What “advice” is. A short playbook for one merchant, appended to the prompt: the checkout’s URL sequence, its known traps, what its confirmation page looks like, and when to ask the human. It comes from what earlier agents saw on that site. The model, tools and permissions are the same in both arms.
  • The paired numbers are the honest ones. The arms aren’t balanced (46 unadvised runs against 34 advised, across several rounds), so the headline rates can drift on merchant mix. Restricting to the 28 tasks that ran both ways gives 20/28 → 24/28: advice fixed 5 runs and broke 1.
  • Approval time is excluded. Every purchase pauses for a human to approve the wallet spend request. Those waits ran from 11 seconds to 10 minutes and aren’t the agent’s doing, so the times here count only the agent’s own work.
  • Caveats. Small n, live commercial sites that change under you, two model versions across rounds. Merchants were picked because agents already struggled there, so the unadvised baseline is not a general “agents can’t check out” rate. All three journeys shown are ones the unadvised run failed — the interesting case, not the typical one. Unadvised runs finished 67% of the time overall.
